# Style orchestration research and upgrade review

This W33 review covers every built-in style. It separates sourced practice from
the arrangement decisions made for Ostinato. Sources favor museums, archives,
educational institutions, and openly inspectable scores. A score link is a
study reference, not musical data copied into the product.

The product has five accompaniment roles, not a literal orchestra: bass, chord
rhythm, melodic answers, backing texture, and drums/percussion. The performer's
accordion remains outside that rack. The goal is therefore to preserve each
style's most recognizable interaction within five roles and leave space for the
player.

## Cross-style audit and implemented priorities

The pre-W33 patterns already had four-bar variation, phrase dynamics, two fills,
and style-shaped intro/ending material. The audit found three larger weaknesses:

1. Several style racks reused flute even where a reed/brass attack defines the
   idiom. W33 adds the pinned CC0 VSCO clarinet and trumpet staccato mappings.
2. The New Orleans/cha-cha profile used acoustic guitar even though both the
   Latin montuno side and the New Orleans rhythm-section evidence support piano.
3. The interface hid density, instrumentation and dynamics, making musically
   distinct patterns appear equivalent. W33 derives one timeline directly from
   renderer onset/accent/gate/mix data for both the designer and live surface.

The following remain intentionally pending rather than faked with misleading
labels: nylon guitar, pedal steel, reggae organ, tenor sax/harmonica, dedicated
Brazilian percussion, and a larger multi-horn rack. Subjective mix balance and
style authenticity require performer listening after deployment.
